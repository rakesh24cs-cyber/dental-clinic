Helpful answers

Frequently Asked Questions

Can medication completely relieve tooth pain?

Medication may provide short-term relief, but lasting relief requires identifying and treating the root cause through professional dental care.

Does teeth scaling cause enamel damage or sensitivity?

No. Scaling safely removes plaque and tartar. Temporary sensitivity can occur, but it usually subsides as the gums heal.

How often should I visit the dentist?

A routine check-up every six months is recommended for preventive care and professional cleaning.

Do all wisdom teeth need to be removed?

No. Removal is only needed if a wisdom tooth is impacted, infected, painful, or damaging nearby teeth.

What is the best age to get braces?

Braces are often most effective between ages 12 and 14, while teeth and jaws are still developing. Adults can benefit too.

Can fluorosis stains be removed through scaling?

Scaling removes surface stains, but fluorosis is an internal stain. Cosmetic treatments such as bleaching, veneers or crowns may be recommended.

Can painful teeth be treated with fillings?

Not always. When decay reaches the inner pulp, root canal treatment or extraction may be necessary. Fillings are suitable when damage is limited to enamel or dentin.

Is a crown necessary after root canal treatment?

Yes. A crown is generally recommended to protect a weakened treated tooth from fracture and provide durability during chewing.

Can milk teeth need root canal treatment?

Yes. A root canal may preserve a milk tooth when it has deep decay and is expected to remain in the mouth for some time.

How can I maintain good oral hygiene?

Brush twice daily with proper technique, rinse after meals, floss regularly and schedule routine dental check-ups.

Is it important to replace missing teeth after extraction?

Yes. Replacement helps maintain bite alignment and prevents nearby teeth from shifting, preserving chewing function.

What are the benefits of dental implants?

Implants restore appearance and function, provide a stable natural-looking replacement and can improve confidence and quality of life.

Which toothbrush and toothpaste should I use?

Use a soft or medium-bristled toothbrush with a small head and a non-abrasive fluoride toothpaste. Replace the toothbrush every three months.

What causes tooth sensitivity?

Common causes include enamel wear, tooth decay, exposed roots and hairline cracks. A dental examination identifies the exact cause.
